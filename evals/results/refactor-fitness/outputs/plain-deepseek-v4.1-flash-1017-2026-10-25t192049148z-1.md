Your instinct is directionally right, but I’d narrow it. “Self-led lifters who track both” is a behavior, not a tight first segment. Your best first customer is:

> **Two-app intermediate lifters in a body-composition phase.**  
> Self-coached lifters in the US/Canada, roughly 25–40, training 3–5x/week on a structured program, already using one workout app (Strong, Hevy, etc.) and one nutrition app (MyFitnessPal, MacroFactor, etc.), currently cutting, bulking, or recomping, and frustrated that the two datasets live apart.

## 1. Who to target first

**Your guess is right, but too broad.** “People who track both and want one app” includes casual users, beginners, and highly advanced lifters with entrenched workflows. The first segment should have *pain, budget, and a reason to act now*.

Target first:

- **Intermediate self-led lifters** — not beginners; they already log consistently.
- **Already using two apps** — you don’t have to teach the logging habit.
- **In a goal phase** — cutting, bulking, or recomping. This is when “should I adjust calories or training?” becomes urgent.
- **Willing to pay for guidance** — because your paid product is AI coaching, not just logging.
- **Reachable in communities** — Reddit, Discord, Facebook groups, fitness creators, and people publicly asking about Strong/Hevy + MyFitnessPal workflows.

Why this segment first: they already have the behavior, understand the tradeoff, can compare you to their current stack, and have a clear “job” for the AI: connect food and lifting data so they make better adjustments.

Do **not** start with:
- Complete beginners who don’t track anything.
- Casual gym-goers who only want workout logging.
- Advanced competitors who already have a coach.
- Pure calorie-counting users who don’t care about strength progression.

## 2. What to say to them

Your current headline — “Nutrition and training should work together” — is a good manifesto, but it’s not a strong acquisition hook. Everyone agrees with it. It doesn’t name the user’s pain or the switching reason.

Lead with **fragmentation and replacement**, not AI. AI is the paid value; “one app instead of two” is the acquisition message.

### Positioning statement

> For lifters who already track macros and training, [App] replaces the two-app juggling act and gives you AI suggestions that connect your food to your lifting — so you can make one clear adjustment at a time. You approve or dismiss every change.

### Landing page / ad copy direction

**Headline:**  
**Stop tracking your lifts in one app and your macros in another.**

**Subhead:**  
One app for lifters who track both. Log meals and workouts in one place, then get AI suggestions that connect your nutrition to your training — approve or dismiss each one.

**Bullets:**
- Log meals by search, barcode, photo, or plain text.
- Log workouts by sets, reps, and weight.
- Get one connected adjustment at a time — e.g., protein, calories, recovery, or training volume.
- Free logging. Paid AI coaching when you want the connection.
- You stay in control: suggestions are approved or dismissed, never forced.

**CTA:**  
**Start free and log your next workout**

### Message angles to test

1. **The replacement angle:** “One app instead of two.”
2. **The connection angle:** “Finally see your macros and lifts together.”
3. **The control angle:** “AI suggests; you decide.”
4. **The outcome angle:** “Stop guessing whether your cut is hurting your lifts.”

### Community/outreach message

Use this in Reddit/Discord/Facebook groups where allowed, or in DMs after genuine engagement:

> I’m building an app for lifters who already track macros and training but hate using two apps. It logs meals and workouts in one place, then an AI coach reads both and suggests one adjustment at a time — you approve or dismiss it. I’m looking for 5–10 people currently using something like Hevy/Strong + MyFitnessPal to try it and tell me if the suggestions are actually useful. Free access, no pitch — I just want honest feedback.

Words to use: *double logging, macros and lifts, cut/recomp/bulk, protein, progression, one app instead of two, approve/dismiss.*  
Avoid: *holistic wellness, optimize your journey, AI-powered, all-in-one platform.*

## 3. What to try this week

Run a **narrow concierge test**, not a broad campaign. The goal is to test whether this ICP feels the pain and whether the connected AI suggestion is valuable.

### Hypothesis

> If we target two-app lifters in a cut/recomp with the message “one app instead of two, with connected AI suggestions,” at least 5–10 qualified users will install, log both a workout and meals, and at least 3 will say the AI suggestion was useful enough to act on.

### This week’s plan

**Monday:** Rewrite your landing page around the new headline and CTA. Add simple tracking: landing page click, App Store/Play Store click, install, first meal logged, first workout logged, both logged, AI suggestion viewed, approved/dismissed, paywall click.

**Tuesday:** Build a list of 30–50 places/people where two-app lifters hang out. Search Reddit/Discord/Facebook/Instagram for phrases like “MyFitnessPal and Hevy,” “track macros and lifting,” “best app for macros and workouts,” “Strong app nutrition,” “cutting and lifting plateau.”

**Wednesday:** Reach out manually to 30 qualified people or post in 3–5 communities where self-promotion is allowed. Offer a concierge onboarding: “I’ll help you set it up and watch you log one workout + one day of meals.”

**Thursday–Friday:** Onboard 5–10 users. Watch them use the app. Do not over-explain. Track where they get stuck. Ask them to review their first AI suggestion and tell you whether it was obvious, useful, confusing, or untrustworthy.

**Weekend:** Interview 5 users. Ask:
- What do you use today for workouts and macros?
- When was the last time you looked at both together to make a decision?
- What did you do? What was annoying about it?
- What would make you switch from your two apps?
- Would you pay $9.99/mo for connected AI suggestions? Why or why not?

### Metrics that matter

Track this funnel:

Landing page visit → install → first meal logged → first workout logged → both logged → first AI suggestion viewed → suggestion approved/dismissed → paywall click/willingness to pay.

Success looks like:
- 30 targeted touches → 10 replies → 5–8 installs.
- At least 5 users log both a workout and a full day of meals.
- At least 3 say the AI suggestion was actionable or trustworthy.
- At least 2 say they’d pay or click the paywall.

Decision rules:
- **If you can’t recruit qualified users:** your ICP or offer is too narrow or not painful enough.
- **If they install but don’t log both:** onboarding/UX problem, not messaging.
- **If they log both but don’t care about AI suggestions:** the paid feature is not valuable enough yet.
- **If they log both and like suggestions:** double down on this ICP and this “one app instead of two” message.

Bottom line: target **two-app lifters actively changing body composition** first. Sell them on **replacing two apps and getting one connected adjustment at a time**. This week, manually recruit 5–10 of them and watch whether the connected AI suggestion creates the “aha” moment.
